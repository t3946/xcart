The ferrules are nickel (aluminium) plated. They are imported by our manufacturer from Japan.

They are seamless!

They are well balanced.

Our manufacturer imports synthetics hair from Japan. Natural hairs are from Russia.

We are shipping from Kingston, Ontario, Canada.

Yes, they are in US dollars.

In the section marked Kolinsky Sable 1001-R Series: Rounds above round # 2/0 is round # 55? What does # 55 mean? Is this a round # 3/0 brush? 
Yes, it is something like # 3/0. We are not sure why our manufacturer calls it # 55. Please, refer to the sizes (length and diameter) - they are of real importance. 
 

I've been looking at your website and am wondering what is the difference between the 1101 series and the 1105? 
1101 series corresponds to the short hair spotting / retouching brushes. 1105 series corresponds to a conventional tuft. 1105 are long-handle brushes whereas 1101 are short-handle ones. 
 

I'm considering purchasing a synthetic # 6 flat brush. My choices, I see, are long handle and short handle. I only do acrylic painting. How long is the short handle; the long handle? I'm looking for something better than a Robert Simmons brush. 
Our synthetic short handle # 6 flat brush is 7.5 inch long. Our long handle brushes are about 8.5 inches. 
 

Being a begineer to oil painting could you advise which brushes are suitable for oils (I have been told hog?) 
Yes, hog (or 'bristle' as they are listed on our web site) brushes are suitable for oil paints. Kolinsky sable brushes are suitable for oil paints as well but you have to clean them properly (you have to take extra care compared to bristle brushes). Also Kolinsky sable brushes are much more expensive. 
 

I require fine art brushes equivalent in hardness to hog hair brushes. 
Our bristle hair brushes are equivalent to hog hair brushes. 
 

I'm wondering about your synthetic brushes. What are they best used for? Do they keep a fine point, like your other brushes? (Do all your brushes keep a fine point?) Are they good for use with acrylic paint? What can you tell me about them, and how do they compare to your other brushes? 
- Yes, our synthetic brushes keep a fine point. Synthetics fiber is specially designed in such a way that its diameter decreases towards the tip. In this property they are similar to Kolinsky sable and Siberian squirrel hairs. As for the bristle hair - its diameter decreases somewhat towards the tip but not as much as Kolinsky hair or synthetics fiber. As a result bristle rounds, for example, never come to a point tip.
- Yes, synthetic brushes are certainly suitable for acrylic paint, see our chart. But for the best results we would recommend Kolinsky sable brushes (they provide better control of the paint flow). 
 

As I understand "size numbers" are not universal ... . 
Size numbers are certainly not universal among different manufacturers. In our case they are different even for different lines of our brushes (different lines of Kolinsky rounds, for example). That is why we provide sizes of each brush both in mm and inches. 
 

I can see the brush sizes in mm on your site but can't relate that to my personal experience. Is your Kolonok brush sizing based on the traditional "English" size scheme, or the "French / International" (smaller than the English for a given size over about a size 3) system? 
Our brush sizing is based on the "French / International" system. But different lines of our brushes have different sizing systems! Therefore, please, check the sizes before you buy a brush. Inch sizes are given in the parenthesis.

Sure, our manufacturer can produce any types of art brushes. But be aware that it could take two or three months and can be quite expensive. If you know exactly what you need, please, feel free to request a quote.
